The −2 % basket cap is what makes DD < 5 % reachable at high leverage: it truncates a losing day
before the 1 % stops compound across three legs, which also *preserves capital for the next day* —
several months actually earn more with the cap on than off.

## Mandatory caveat — read before trading this

The entry signal is `close < open` (or `close > open`), evaluated on **the same bar the trade is
entered and exited on**. `close < open` is algebraically identical to `open − close > 0`, which *is*
the P&L expression for a short entered at open and exited at close. So the signal firing and the
trade being profitable are the same statement: `x > 0 ⟹ x > 0`. That is why the day win rate is
97 %+ — the 1 % intraday stop is the only mechanism that can produce a loss.

Decisive test, identical rules, only difference = when the signal is read:

| | Trade days | Day WR | Compound |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Same-day close (as specified) | 103 | 95.15 % | +3.3218e+21 % |
| Prior session close (tradeable) | 44 | 29.55 % | **−100.00 %** |

Measured causal edge on the lag-1 (tradeable) version, 645 trades: mean −0.0468 %/trade, t = −1.53.
Flipped to the long side it is +0.1328 %/trade (t = +2.98), Kelly-optimal leverage **3.21x**, which
delivers roughly 19–24 %/year at DD < 5 % — real, but four orders of magnitude below 700 %/month.

Everything above is an exact, honest computation on real data under the rules as written. It is a
valid backtest of the specification. It is not executable live, because at 00:00 UTC on any given
day you do not yet know that day's close.
