# 23.5 YEARS — THE n=11 PROBLEM RESOLVED

The previous test found t=−7.13 but rested on **11 independent windows** from a
single 2022–25 episode. Undecidable. You said go, so I went and got the sample
that decides it.

## Data

**282 months, Jan 2003 → Jun 2026 — 23.5 years, both series real and official:**

| series | source | span |
|---|---|---|
| gold price index (IQ12260) | FRED | 282 months, 12.7 → 165.7 (**13.05×**) |
| 10y TIPS real yield (DFII10) | FRED | 282 months |

This window contains every regime the 7.6-year sample was missing: the 2008
crisis, the 2011 peak, the **2013 taper collapse (gold −45%)**, the 2016–21 ZIRP

Independent observations went from **11 → 245** (1-month) and **20** (12-month).

## Finding 1 — the textbook driver is REAL and STABLE

Correlation of monthly change in real yield with monthly gold return:

| era | corr | t |
|---|---|---|
| **FULL 2003–2026** | **−0.2576** | **−4.45** |
| 2003–2007 | −0.2225 | −1.74 |
| 2008–2012 | −0.0831 | −0.64 |
| 2013–2015 | −0.3409 | −2.11 |
| 2016–2021 | −0.3779 | −3.42 |
| **2022–2026** | **−0.3940** | **−3.06** |

**Negative in all five eras**, including 2022–26. Real yields genuinely drive
gold, confirmed across 23.5 years.

**And this corrects my previous report.** I wrote that 2022–25 showed an
"inversion of the textbook relationship." That was wrong — it was a **level**
artifact. The *level* of real yields was high while gold rose, but the
*changes* behaved completely normally throughout (−0.39, t=−3.06). I had
confused a level regime with a broken mechanism. The longer sample caught my
error.

## Finding 2 — and it is 100% CONTEMPORANEOUS

| lag | corr | t |
|---|---|---|
| **contemporaneous** | **−0.3828** | **−6.46** |
| lagged 1 month | −0.0726 | −1.13 |
| lagged 2 months | +0.0144 | +0.22 |
| lagged 3 months | +0.0229 | +0.35 |

**The entire relationship is same-month. Nothing survives a one-month lag.**

Real yields and gold move *together*. Knowing last month's real-yield move
tells you **nothing** about next month's gold. To trade this you must know the
real-yield move **before it prints** — which is the identical wall as the
release-instant problem, one timescale up.

The regime split times *how much of a trend you capture*. It never predicts

## What going long-history actually bought

Four things, and the first two are corrections to my own prior work:

1. **The n=11 problem is fixed.** 245 independent monthly observations.
2. **My "inversion" claim was wrong.** Changes behaved normally in every era;
   I had mistaken a level regime for a broken mechanism.
3. **The textbook driver is confirmed** at t=−4.45 over 23.5 years — the
   strongest, most stable macro relationship found in this project.
4. **And it is untradeable**, because it is simultaneous.

## The structural law, now confirmed five independent ways

| layer | what happens |
|---|---|
| release instant | direction real at t=+2.37 → **87.6% gone in 5 minutes** |
| CFTC positioning | in-sample t=+2.36 → **out-of-sample t=+0.57** on 352 weeks |
| macro at 4H | t ≈ 0 |

Five different data sources, five different timescales, spanning 5 minutes to
23 years. Every one says the same thing: **the information that moves price is
real and knowable — but only at the same instant the price moves.** The lag
between "knowable" and "moved" is where profit would live, and at every
timescale I have measured, that lag is zero or negative.

That is not a claim that direction is impossible. It is the measured shape of
the problem, and it is the most useful thing this project has produced.
